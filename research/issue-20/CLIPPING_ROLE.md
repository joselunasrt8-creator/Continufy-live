# Issue 20: frozen clipping-role decision

**Protocol:** `continufy-ai-content-paid-pilot-v1.0.0`  
**Frozen before outreach:** 2026-09-25  
**Readiness determination:** `READY_TO_TEST_CLIPPING_AS_SUPPORTING_TRANSFORMATION`

This decision makes clipping a bounded component of the first paid-service offer.
It does not make clipping the company identity, establish customer demand, or
change the frozen pilot protocol.

## First-offer role

The first offer tests **H2 — supporting transformation**.

A customer-owned or legitimately licensed source may yield at most **two
short-form video assets** inside the existing 1–8 asset pilot scope. Each short
must be a materially transformed derivative that helps make an expert idea,
demonstration, or story easier to understand or harder to ignore. The package
must still contain at least one other permitted asset form, as required by the
frozen protocol.

Customer-facing language uses **short-form story derivatives** or **cinematic
short-form derivatives**. It does not market generic clipping, clip volume,
automatic virality, or pay-per-view performance.

## Ordinary versus materially transformed

An **ordinary clip** is principally an excerpt: selecting a source interval,
trimming it, reframing it, normalizing it, and adding basic captions or branding.
It is commodity fulfillment and is not the proposed differentiator.

A **cinematic short-form derivative** materially adds at least two of:

- a newly structured hook or narrative arc;
- original or properly licensed visual explanation of hard-to-film subject matter;
- contextual graphics, diagrams, or sourced supporting material;
- a new voiceover or written framing approved for the customer;
- an explicit path to an authorized longer asset, offer, or next action.

Cosmetic filters, automated subtitles, aspect-ratio conversion, and generic
stock footage do not by themselves make a derivative cinematic.

## Prospective selection and approval

Before production outcomes are visible, a human selects candidate moments from
the eligible source using this order:

1. relevance to one audience problem or valuable expert claim;
2. ability to stand alone without distorting the source;
3. need for visualization or context beyond an ordinary excerpt;
4. rights, factual, brand, and platform safety;
5. fit with the frozen package and named destinations.

Record the selected source ranges and reasons before transformation. AI may
propose moments, hooks, visuals, or drafts, but a human performs final selection,
reviews the complete derivative, and controls approval. Publication remains
separately authorized and manual under the frozen protocol.

## Fields required for the pilot

For every proposed short-form derivative preserve:

- source range, lineage, selection reason, and transformation classification;
- human and AI contribution by stage;
- active labor, waiting, generation, review, revision, and approval time;
- itemized direct tool, API, media, contractor, and labor cost;
- rights basis for every added visual, voice, music, likeness, and source;
- customer acceptance and usefulness;
- whether the derivative affected purchase or rejection;
- whether the customer would buy or continue without short-form derivatives;
- fixed-window performance and attributable downstream outcomes only where
  prospectively authorized.

Views remain attention observations. They are not customer value, attributable
revenue, or evidence that clipping caused an outcome.

## Acquisition questions

Issue #12 should record voluntary answers, when available, to:

1. Did the short-form derivatives make the first package easier to understand or buy?
2. Was cinematic visualization material to the decision, or would ordinary editing suffice?
3. Would the customer accept the same package without short-form derivatives?
4. Is the customer comparing this offer primarily on per-clip price?
5. Which result would justify a separate paid continuation?

Negative, missing, and refusal evidence must remain intact.

## Falsification

The H2 role is weakened or contradicted if prospective evidence shows any of:

- purchase and continuation do not depend on the short-form derivatives;
- customers value only the lowest per-clip price;
- an automated clipper or ordinary editor produces an acceptable equivalent;
- enhancement adds labor, cost, or revisions without additional usefulness;
- short-form attention produces no meaningful downstream behavior;
- rights or provenance cannot be maintained; or
- another permitted asset form creates more value for less burden.

After legitimate evidence, classify the role only with one of Issue #20's
post-evidence determinations. Until then, this file establishes readiness to test,
not that clipping supports the differentiated offer.

## Boundaries

- Experimental proposed price remains $250–$400.
- The source, 1–8 asset, two-form, three-destination, and two-revision limits remain unchanged.
- Pure pay-per-view compensation is not authorized.
- No generic AI clipper or other software is authorized.
- Owned-media distribution remains governed separately by Issues #17 and #21.
