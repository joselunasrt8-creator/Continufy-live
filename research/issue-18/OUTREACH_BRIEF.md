# Issue 18: differentiated paid-service outreach brief

**Protocol:** `continufy-ai-content-paid-pilot-v1.0.0`  
**Frozen before outreach:** 2026-09-25  
**Clipping dependency:** `research/issue-20/CLIPPING_ROLE.md`  
**Determination:** `READY_FOR_DIFFERENTIATED_OUTREACH`

This brief freezes a specific offer and falsification surface for manual testing
through Issue #12. It does not establish demand, differentiation, results, or
product-market fit.

## Primary ideal customer

A creator-led business, subject-matter expert, or founder who:

- already records podcasts, interviews, webinars, demonstrations, presentations,
  or expert sessions;
- owns or legitimately licenses one complete source;
- sells a real product, service, membership, sponsorship, or affiliate offer;
- has an audience or credible distribution path;
- has valuable ideas or stories that are difficult or expensive to visualize;
- lacks the time or workflow to turn one source into a coherent multi-format campaign;
- can approve work and provide the frozen measurement permissions.

The first outreach cohort should exclude prospects who only want commodity clip
volume, cannot establish source authority, have no credible distribution or
value path, or require revenue or virality guarantees.

## Persistent job to be done

> Turn valuable expertise trapped in a long recording into a small, coherent,
> human-reviewed content campaign that makes difficult ideas visible and gives
> the customer enough operational and outcome evidence to decide whether to
> continue paying.

## One-sentence offer

> For **$300**, we turn one authorized expert recording into a human-reviewed,
> cinematic multi-format campaign for up to three named destinations, then
> measure production burden and authorized downstream outcomes so you can make a
> grounded continuation decision.

The exact 1–8 asset package, at least two permitted forms, destinations, source,
baseline, permissions, and two-revision limit are frozen per customer through
Issue #12. A suitable initial package may contain two cinematic short-form
derivatives plus two independently reviewable written or packaging assets.
Clipping follows Issue #20 and is not sold as the differentiator. Funds must
settle before production begins.

## Before and after

| Current state | Pilot intervention |
| --- | --- |
| Valuable ideas remain buried in one long recording | Human selects a bounded campaign angle and source ranges |
| Ordinary clips depend on the original footage alone | Difficult ideas may receive original or properly licensed cinematic visualization |
| Separate outputs are improvised | One frozen package connects short-form and written/packaging assets |
| AI output may bypass factual or brand judgment | Humans control selection, complete review, approval, and publication |
| Time, revisions, and tool costs are unknown | Labor, waiting, rework, and direct cost are recorded |
| Views are treated as success | Attention, attributable commercial outcomes, usefulness, and repeat payment remain distinct |
| One-off delivery ends the learning loop | A 30-day continuation decision is preserved |

## What is differentiated

The positioning hypothesis is the combination of:

1. selecting hard-to-visualize expert knowledge rather than maximizing generic output;
2. applying editorial and story judgment to one coherent campaign;
3. using cinematic explanation only where an ordinary excerpt cannot show the idea;
4. producing multiple permitted forms from one authorized source;
5. keeping factual, brand, rights, approval, and publication control human;
6. measuring production economics and legitimately observable outcomes; and
7. ending with a paid-continuation decision.

No single AI tool, prompt, editing action, or content format is claimed as
defensible by itself.

## Commodity and easily copied parts

Moment suggestions, transcription, captions, reframing, silence removal, basic
hooks, stock footage, resizing, title drafts, and generic editing are commodity
capabilities. They may be used, but they cannot support the purchase claim.

## Alternatives and desk falsification

| Alternative | When it wins | What the pilot must prove prospectively |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do nothing | Content is not an important constraint | The customer values release of the selected knowledge enough to pay and consider continuation |
| Prompt a model internally | The customer has time, judgment, and production skill | The governed end-to-end burden is lower or usefulness is higher |
| Automated AI clipper | Ordinary excerpts satisfy the need | Cinematic transformation adds material usefulness without destroying economics |
| Conventional editor | Visualization and measurement add little | The integrated story, visualization, and evidence loop affects purchase or continuation |
| Low-cost freelancer | Lowest price is the deciding factor | The customer values the bounded operation above commodity output |

This comparison exposes the hypothesis to rejection. It is not evidence that the
pilot wins.

## Minimum proof by claim

| Claim | Minimum acceptable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The problem exists | Prospect describes a current production or visualization burden |
| The offer is understandable | Prospect can restate the job and bounded package without generic-AI framing |
| The package is worth $300 | Signed acceptance plus settled payment |
| Visualization matters | Voluntary purchase/rejection reason and post-delivery usefulness evidence |
| Burden changed | Prospectively frozen baseline and recorded pilot labor/rework |
| Content produced attention | Authorized fixed-window platform evidence |
| Content produced commercial value | Prospectively configured deterministic attribution and settled net revenue |
| The service should continue | Separate settled follow-on payment |
| A software opportunity exists | Issue #19 scale and repeated-friction gates |

## Permitted claims

Manual outreach may say:

- the offer is an experimental $300 paid pilot;
- one authorized source becomes a bounded multi-format package;
- AI assists selected production stages;
- humans control review, approval, rights checks, and publication;
- labor, cost, revision burden, usefulness, and authorized outcomes will be measured;
- the pilot is intended to support a continuation decision.

## Prohibited claims

Do not promise virality, views, leads, sales, revenue, causal lift, time savings,
superior quality, passive income, guaranteed ROI, autonomous publishing, or a
validated proprietary system. Do not present public creator results, synthetic
fixtures, protocol readiness, or AI capability as customer evidence.

## Likely objections and evidence-bounded responses

- **“I can use an AI clipper.”** That may be sufficient. This pilot tests whether
  story selection, visualization, multi-format packaging, and human control add
  enough value to justify the difference.
- **“Why not hire an editor?”** A conventional editor may be the better option.
  The experiment measures whether an integrated campaign and decision record are
  more useful for this source.
- **“Can you guarantee performance?”** No. The pilot measures outcomes that the
  customer legitimately authorizes; it does not guarantee distribution or revenue.
- **“Why pay before results?”** The payment covers the frozen production scope.
  Performance compensation is not part of v1.
- **“Can you post for me?”** Only a separately authorized human manual publication
  may occur. Service acceptance and payment do not grant publishing authority.
- **“Can I get a free sample?”** Free production would not test the frozen paid
  offer. The prospect may review the scope and process, then accept or decline.

## Purchase and rejection reasons to capture

Issue #12 should preserve voluntary, evidence-bounded reasons including:

- visualization need;
- multi-format convenience;
- story/editorial judgment;
- governed review or rights control;
- measurement and continuation loop;
- price;
- preference for ordinary clipping or editing;
- ability to do the work internally;
- lack of distribution or monetization path;
- source-authority or measurement concerns;
- timing, trust, scope, or no current need.

## Falsification conditions

Conclude `NO_DEFENSIBLE_DIFFERENTIATOR_IDENTIFIED` or revise only through a new
prospective version if evidence shows that:

- eligible prospects primarily value generic editing or lowest per-clip price;
- cinematic visualization is immaterial to purchase and continuation;
- added visualization raises cost or revisions without usefulness;
- customers cannot distinguish the package from an ordinary clipper or editor;
- the same result is obtainable through a materially simpler alternative;
- $250–$400 is repeatedly rejected by prospects with the stated problem;
- purchase depends on prohibited performance promises; or
- continuation requires work outside the frozen scope.

Negative and null outcomes remain evidence. `READY_FOR_DIFFERENTIATED_OUTREACH`
means only that the offer is specific enough for manual market testing through
Issue #12.
